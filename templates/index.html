Breast Self-Exam Awareness

A breast self-exam helps women understand how their breasts normally look and feel. Regular self-checks can help identify unusual changes early.

Early detection can improve treatment success and increase awareness about breast health.

Start Health Assessment

How Often Should You Check?

  • Perform breast self-exams once every month.
  • Women with periods should check a few days after periods end.
  • Post-menopausal women should choose the same day each month.
  • Self-awareness helps notice unusual changes early.

3 Simple Self-Exam Steps

1. In The Shower

Check your breast and underarm area gently using your fingers.

2. Mirror Check

Look for swelling, skin changes or unusual breast shape.

3. Lying Down

Move fingers gently around the breast checking for hard areas.

Early Detection Saves Lives

According to the American Cancer Society, when breast cancer is detected early and remains localized, the 5-year relative survival rate is over 99%.

Early detection includes monthly breast self-exams, regular clinical breast exams, mammograms, and maintaining healthy lifestyle habits.

Signs & Symptoms

Symptoms may include breast lumps, nipple tenderness, skin changes, swelling, redness, or discharge.

Breast Pain & Lump

Breast pain or lumps can occur for many reasons. Some may not be cancerous, but should still be checked by professionals.

Breast Cyst

A breast cyst is a fluid-filled sac that may feel like a soft lump. Medical evaluation helps identify the cause.

Clinical Breast Exam

Healthcare professionals perform physical and visual examinations to help identify unusual changes early.

Mammogram

Mammograms are breast X-rays used to detect suspicious areas before symptoms become noticeable.

Healthy Habits

Eating healthy foods, exercising regularly, maintaining healthy weight, and reducing stress support better breast health.

Important Medical Note

Breast self-exams are useful for awareness, but they should not replace mammograms or professional medical checkups.

If you notice lumps, swelling, discharge, pain, or unusual skin changes, consult a healthcare professional immediately.
